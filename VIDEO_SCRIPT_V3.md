# Round — video script, version 3

**"Nobody Signs It"**
Runtime 2:30 · Format 16:9 · Ensemble, four rooms · Fast cut, sparse voice

---

## Where this sits against V1 and V2

| | **V1 — The Line She Stopped At** | **V2 — Nobody Counted the Days** | **V3 — Nobody Signs It** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Engine** | Being *understood* in your own language | Being *welcomed back* after you disappear | Being *carried* by people who take no credit |

V3 keeps V2's tempo and ensemble structure — the thing you liked — and swaps
the subject entirely. No one falls behind. Nobody is compared to anybody. The
whole film runs on one feature the other two never touch.

---

## Why this feature carries a film

From `src/lib/prayer-requests.ts`, verbatim from the module's own rules:

- **Private by default.** Creation always writes `visibility = "private"`.
  Sharing is a separate, explicit, per-request action with its own confirmation.
- **Count, never names.** *"pray() writes an act row; nothing in this module
  ever returns who prayed. The only thing that leaves here is an integer."*
- **Author-owned answered.** Only the author can mark a request answered.
- **Escalation first.** The gate runs before a single row exists. If it can't
  produce a verdict, nothing is written at all.

So the product genuinely does this: you can ask for help without performing, put
it in front of four people by choice, be prayed for by three of them — and never
find out which three. **Nobody signs it.** That is the film.

---

## Before you shoot

**All four are dramatizations** performed by actors; the end card says so and
must not be cut. Every screen is the real product at
https://yv-gloo-chalange.vercel.app. The private/shared toggle, the confirmation
step, the count, and the *Answered* state are all real — shoot them, mock
nothing up.

**Do not name the illness.** Amara's mother is having surgery. That is all the
audience gets, and all they need. The moment you specify a diagnosis the film
becomes about that disease instead of about being carried.

**Nobody cries.** Not Amara, not Henry, not anyone. The film earns its emotion
by withholding; a single tear anywhere and it collapses into a charity appeal.

---

## The story in one line

A woman asks four strangers for help, three of them answer, and she will never
know which three.

## Production notes

**Casting.** Amara carries the film almost wordlessly and must be able to play
*relief* — the hardest emotion to act without sentimentality. Henry, Sofia and
Dev each have under six seconds; cast faces, not CVs.

**The number must be shot practically.** Screen-record the real count changing
in the app, then composite. Do not animate a fake counter — the whole film is an
argument that this specific product behaves this specific way.

**Sofia speaks Spanish** and her message reaches Dev in English through the
product's own translation, with the *Translated by Round* label visible. Two
seconds, but it is the only place the multilingual layer appears in V3.

**Silence at 1:18 is a real cut to nothing.** No room tone, no bed. Four to six
frames of true silence before the pillow shot. It should feel like a held
breath.

**Colour.** Cool and slightly desaturated from 0:00 to 1:18. Warmth enters with
the melody at 1:18 and builds. The hospital corridor is the brightest shot in
the film — daylight, not fluorescent.

**Music.** One low note per character in the open; single-instrument phrase at
0:36; full melody at 1:18; unhurried and warm through the ending. No lyrics, no
worship-track cliché, no swell under the *Answered* tap — let the tap be small.

**What NOT to show.** No named illness. No tears. No group video call — these
four never meet, never see each other's faces, and that is the film's entire
thesis. No reveal of which three prayed, not even a knowing glance. If the
audience can work it out, the edit has failed.

## 60-second cutdown

Keep: 0:00–0:16 (four lies), 0:56–1:18 (the date and the confirmation),
1:18–1:44 (one, two, three), the *Answered* tap, end card.

Drop: the reciprocal tap for Dev, the hospital corridor, the final kitchen line.

The cutdown loses the reciprocity — that she gives before she asks — which is
what stops the full film from being about a woman receiving charity. Keep the
full version if you can.

## If this is the Kaggle submission video

**Recommendation:** run V3 at **2:10** by tightening the 0:36–0:56 circle
montage and trimming the ending to the *Answered* tap, then append **45 seconds**
of silent screen capture — Instant Access → passage → reflection → digest →
Agent Console. Total ~2:55.

**V3 versus V2 for the submission.** V2 argues a product philosophy; V3
demonstrates a product mechanism. V3 is the stronger choice if you want a judge
to remember one specific thing Round does that nothing else does — *prayed for
by a count, never by a name* is unusual enough to survive a day of watching
demos. V2 is the stronger choice if you want a judge to nod along at a design
principle.

One practical advantage: **V3 has no adjacency risk.** It takes no position on
streaks, habit tracking, or anything the YouVersion Bible App does. It is purely
additive — a thing that happens in the room around the reading.
