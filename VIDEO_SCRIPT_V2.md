# Round — video script, version 2

**"Nobody Counted the Days"**
Runtime 2:40 · Format 16:9 · Ensemble, four voices · The thread narrates

---

## How this differs from version 1

Deliberately built on a different emotional engine so the two are worth
comparing rather than blending.

| | **V1 — "The Line She Stopped At"** | **V2 — "Nobody Counted the Days"** |
|---|---|---|
| **Protagonist** | One woman, interior, alone | Four people, a circle, intercut |
| **Emotional engine** | Being *understood* in your own language | Being *welcomed back* after you disappear |
| **The enemy** | Isolation | Shame |
| **Narration** | Spanish voiceover monologue | The circle thread itself — messages on screen |
| **Tone** | Melancholy → warmth, slow | Wry, warm, faster cutting, one laugh |
| **Entry path shown** | Instant Access, anonymous | Sign in with YouVersion, highlight import |
| **Features shown** | Pre-reading prompts, RVES passage, Escalation card, translation, digest overlap | Icebreaker, AI matching, Reminder, Companion, shared prayer, *the absence of streaks* |
| **Peak moment** | "Marisol and Tom both stopped at the same line." | A man opens an app after nine days and nothing punishes him. |
| **Risk** | Slow open; may lose a judge in the first 20s | Ensemble means less depth per character |

**Feature coverage is almost non-overlapping.** If you shoot both, you cover
nearly the whole product. If you shoot one, V1 is the more distinctive film and
V2 is the more *persuasive* one — it argues against the thing everyone already
hates about habit apps.

---

## Before you shoot

**All four members are dramatizations** performed by actors. The end card says
so and must not be cut. Every screen is the real product at
https://yv-gloo-chalange.vercel.app — shoot the live app, mock nothing up.

**The design constraint is the story.** Round has no leaderboard, no streak
counter, no per-member progress bar, no days-read count. That is a documented
product rule, not a missing feature — and this film is 2:40 of argument for why.
Do not show a streak even ironically.

---

## The story in one line

A man falls nine days behind, dreads opening the app, opens it — and nothing
punishes him.

## Script

### 0:00 – 0:18 — Four strangers, one Tuesday

| | |
|---|---|
| **VISUAL** | Four rooms, hard cuts, 4 seconds each. **PRIYA** (29, Birmingham) reading on a train. **DAVID** (41, Leeds) in hospital scrubs eating a sandwich in a stairwell. **GRACE** (67, Cardiff) at a kitchen table with reading glasses. **TOMÁS** (35, Lisbon) on a balcony at dusk. All four holding phones. None of them know each other. |
| **ON SCREEN (text messages appearing as typed)** | *Day 1.* |
| **SOUND** | Light rhythmic bed. Four notes, one per person. |

> **Direction:** No dialogue for the first 18 seconds. Establish four separate
> lives, four separate rooms, one shared object.

---

### 0:18 – 0:38 — The blank thread problem

| | |
|---|---|
| **VISUAL** | David's phone. He has joined a circle. The thread is empty. He types "Hi everyone!" — looks at it — deletes it. Types "Hey" — deletes it. Locks the phone. |
| **VO (David, dry)** | Every group I've ever joined started with someone saying "let's all introduce ourselves." <br><br> And then nobody does. |
| **SOUND** | Rhythm drops out entirely. Silence on "nobody does." |

---

### 0:38 – 1:00 — Round goes first

| | |
|---|---|
| **VISUAL** | The phone lights up in his hand. A message in the thread, from **Round**. Camera reads it with him. It references something Priya wrote when she joined, and something *he* wrote — his actual answer about why he came. Not a template. His words. |
| **VISUAL (cont.)** | Cut to Priya on the train reading the same message. She looks up. Types immediately. |
| **VO (David)** | It didn't ask us to introduce ourselves. <br><br> It already knew why we came. |
| **SOUND** | Rhythm returns, fuller. |

> **Product truth:** the icebreaker is generated from two members' real
> onboarding answers and posted as a system message attributed to "Round." There
> is deliberately no blank "say hi" state.

---

### 1:00 – 1:20 — Nine days

| | |
|---|---|
| **VISUAL** | Montage, accelerating and darkening. David: a night shift. A second night shift. A phone charging face-down. A takeaway container. A phone face-down again. Days blurring. The app icon on his home screen, untouched, drifting past under his thumb. |
| **ON SCREEN** | *Day 12.* → *Day 15.* → *Day 21.* |
| **VO (David)** | I missed a day. <br><br> Then I missed nine. |
| **SOUND** | Rhythm becomes mechanical, unpleasant. Fluorescent hum. |

---

### 1:20 – 1:36 — The dread

| | |
|---|---|
| **VISUAL** | Night. His car in a hospital car park, engine off. He holds the phone. Thumb hovers over the Round icon. Doesn't tap. Close on his face — this is the real emotion of the film, and it is not sadness. It is **embarrassment**. |
| **VO (David)** | I knew exactly what was waiting. <br><br> A little red number. A broken streak. Some cartoon being disappointed in me. |
| **VO (cont., quieter)** | That's why I didn't open it for nine days. Not because I was busy. |
| **SOUND** | Everything drops to the hum. |

> **Direction:** Hold the hover for a full 3 seconds. Uncomfortably long. The
> whole film pays off against this shot.

---

### 1:36 – 2:00 — Nothing punishes him

| | |
|---|---|
| **VISUAL** | He taps. The app opens. **Camera holds wide on the screen and does not cut for four full seconds.** No streak counter. No red badge. No progress bar. No "you missed 9 days." |
| **VISUAL (cont.)** | One notification, in plain language: a short note about the passage he missed and what the circle has been talking about. Then the thread — and a message from Round that picks up something Grace said, still open, still going. |
| **VISUAL (cont.)** | Then Grace's message, sent six days earlier: *"Leaving this here for whenever you're back, David. No rush."* |
| **VO (David)** | Nothing. <br><br> Nobody had counted. |
| **SOUND** | Hum cuts out. Single warm chord. |

> **Product truth:** the Reminder Agent writes a personalised nudge referencing
> the missed passage and what the circle discussed — never a streak, never a
> comparison. The Companion Agent keeps a quiet thread alive. No pace or streak
> comparison between members is a hard design constraint.

## Production notes

**Casting.** David carries the film and must be able to play embarrassment
without self-pity — this is a man who feels stupid, not tragic. Cast a comic
actor and direct him down. Grace needs warmth with zero sentimentality; her two
lines are the thesis. Priya and Tomás are largely visual.

**The four-second hold at 1:36 is the whole film.** Do not cut it shorter in the
edit. The absence of a punishment only registers if the audience is given time
to look for one and not find it. Test it on someone: if they don't lean in, the
hold is too short.

**Languages.** Tomás writes Portuguese, Grace reads it in English via the
in-product translation. Shoot the real *Show original* toggle. Her laugh at the
Portuguese is the film's only joke — keep it.

**Colour.** Neutral for the four-room open. Sickly fluorescent green-grey
through the nine-days montage and the car park. Warmth snaps in at 1:36 with the
chord and stays.

**Music.** Four-note motif introduced one note per character in the open;
fragmented and mechanised during the montage; reassembled in full at 2:00. No
lyrics.

**What NOT to show.** No streak counters, no leaderboards, no progress bars, no
"Day 47 🔥", no confetti. Not even in the montage, not even as a joke about
another app. The film's argument dies if the audience sees one.

## 60-second cutdown

Keep: 0:18–0:38 (blank thread), 1:20–1:36 (the dread), 1:36–2:00 (nothing
punishes him), end card. Drop the four-room open, the nine-day montage, and the
ensemble payoff.

The cutdown is arguably stronger than the full version — the dread-to-relief
turn is the whole idea, and everything else is context.

---

## If this is the Kaggle submission video

Same tradeoff as V1: this is an ad, and judges also need to see it run.

**Recommendation:** cut to **2:10** by trimming the four-room open to 10 seconds
and tightening the montage, then append **45 seconds** of silent screen capture —
Instant Access → passage → reflection → digest → Agent Console. Total ~2:55.

**Which version to shoot.** V2 is the safer submission film: it opens on
movement rather than stillness, it argues a point a judge can immediately agree
with, and its feature coverage skews toward the agent behaviours that are
hardest to convey in a writeup. V1 is the better *film* and the more memorable
one, but it asks for 22 seconds of patience before it gives anything back.
