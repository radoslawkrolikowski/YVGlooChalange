# Round — video script, version 4

**"Open the Curtains"**
Runtime 2:35 · Format 16:9 · One protagonist · Light is the story

---

## The idea

A woman tries to read the Bible alone in a dark room and gives up. She joins a
circle. The room gets brighter — literally, shot by shot — until she is reading
with the curtains open and can't wait to talk about it.

**The single rule that makes this film work: the light never resets.** Every cut
is brighter than the cut before it, from near-black at 0:00 to direct daylight at
2:20. No exceptions, no dips for drama. The audience will not consciously notice
it and will feel it completely.

---

## Where this sits against V1–V3

| | **V1** | **V2** | **V3** | **V4 — Open the Curtains** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Engine** | Being understood in your language | Being welcomed back | Being carried anonymously | **Alone with a book you don't understand → in a room with people** |
| **Structure** | Interior monologue | Ensemble, four rooms | Ensemble, one mechanism | **One arc, one person, before/after** |
| **Setup needed** | Some | Some | Some | **None. It reads in three seconds.** |
| **Features** | RVES passage, Escalation, translation, digest | Icebreaker, matching, Reminder, Companion | Prayer requests | **Icebreaker, pre-reading prompts, passage, lesson summary, thread, translation, digest** |
| **Risk** | Slow open | Adjacency to streaks | One feature carries everything | **Familiar shape — must be executed beautifully to not feel like a template** |

V4 is the most **legible** of the four and shows the most product. It is also
the most conventional, which means the craft has to carry it: the light
discipline, the sound design, and the dust are what stop it being a stock
before/after.

---

## Before you shoot

**Anna and the circle members are dramatizations** performed by actors. The end
card says so and must not be cut.

**Every screen is the real product** at https://yv-gloo-chalange.vercel.app. The
icebreaker, the pre-reading prompts, the passage with its version chip, the
lesson summary card, the thread, the *Translated by Round* label and the digest
are all live. Screen-record the real app and composite into the in-hand shots.

**She is not a bad person who becomes good.** She is a person who wanted
something and couldn't do it alone. No guilt arc, no redemption beat, no
implication that she was failing. Direct her as *tired*, never as *lapsed*.

---

## The story in one line

She didn't stop reading because she stopped caring. She stopped because there
was nobody to ask.

## Script

### 0:00 – 0:26 — The dark room

| | |
|---|---|
| **VISUAL** | Near-black. A single lamp, low. **ANNA** (33) sits on the end of her bed, coat still on. A Bible on the nightstand — closed, a fine layer of dust visible along the top edge in the lamplight. Dust motes hanging in the beam. |
| **VISUAL (cont.)** | She picks it up. Wipes the top edge with her thumb — one small, honest gesture. Opens it. Reads. |
| **VISUAL (cont.)** | Close on her eyes tracking a line. Back up. Tracking the same line again. A small frown. She reaches for her phone to look something up — stops — puts the phone down. |
| **VISUAL (cont.)** | She reads three more seconds. Then closes the book with her thumb still in it, holds it a moment, and puts it back on the nightstand. Thumb slides out. |
| **VO (Anna)** | I didn't understand it. <br><br> And there was nobody to ask. |
| **SOUND** | Room tone only. A radiator ticking. No music. |

> **Direction:** The whole film depends on this beat not being about guilt. She
> is not avoiding God. She hit a wall in a text with no one to turn to and did
> the reasonable thing. Play it as *fatigue*, and let the closed book sit on the
> nightstand for a full two seconds before you cut.

### 0:26 – 0:42 — Someone sends her a link

| | |
|---|---|
| **VISUAL** | Days later, judging by the light — still dim, but morning-dim now rather than night-dim. Anna on a bus. Her phone buzzes: a message from a friend. Three words and a link. *"This one's different."* |

### 1:24 – 1:50 — This time she has something to hold

| | |
|---|---|
| **VISUAL** | Her flat, next morning. The lamp is off — she is reading by window light now, curtains half open. Two **pre-reading prompts** on her phone: things to notice, a question to hold. She reads them, then opens the passage. Version chip visible beside the reference. |
| **VISUAL (cont.)** | Her thumb drags across a line. The highlight blooms. |
| **VISUAL (cont.)** | She taps the **lesson summary** card open — the passage's main teaching, in plain modern language. Close on her face: the specific, physical relief of a thing making sense. |
| **VISUAL (cont.)** | She reaches for the paper Bible on the nightstand. Opens it flat beside the phone. Both open at once. |
| **VO (Anna)** | Same book. <br><br> I just wasn't on my own with it any more. |
| **SOUND** | Full melody arrives here for the first time. |

> **Direction:** The paper Bible coming back is the emotional hinge of the film.
> Round does not replace it — it gets her back to it. Shoot the two open books
> in one frame and hold it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **VISUAL** | Fast, warm, accelerating. She writes a reflection — a long one, not a polite one. Replies come back. She's answering Priya from a bus stop, from a queue, from a desk. Marcus answers at a kitchen table. João answers from a balcony. |

| **VISUAL (cont.)** | Cut wide: her flat in daylight. Curtains fully open. Window cracked. Street noise coming in. |
| **VO (Anna)** | I started looking forward to it. <br><br> Not the reading. The *arguing about it*. |

| | |

> will feel it.

## Production notes

**Light plan — the spine of the film.** Every stage brighter than the last, no
resets:

| Beat | Source | Level |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 dark room | One practical lamp, low | Near-black |
| 0:26 bus | Overcast window light | Dim, cold |
| 0:42 onboarding | Phone screen on her face | First warm source |
| 1:04 circle | Bus window, brighter | Cool but rising |
| 1:24 reading | Window light, curtains half | Warm, soft |
| 1:50 conversation | Daylight, curtains full | Bright |
| 2:14 end | Direct sun, window open | Brightest frame in the film |

**Colour.** Cool and desaturated through 0:42. Warmth enters with the phone
screen and increases every beat. The final shot is the only one with direct
sunlight.

**Sound design carries as much as the light.** Silence and a ticking radiator at
0:00. One note at 0:26. Two at 0:42. Rhythm at 1:04. Melody at 1:24. Full at
1:50. And at 2:14 — open the window and let real street noise in. The world
arriving in the room is the sound version of the whole film.

**The dust.** Practical, not post. Cornflour and a hard backlight. Present in
the opening beam, absent from the closing one.

**Casting.** Anna must be able to play "tired but not defeated" and then, later,
*enthusiasm* without cheesiness — the hardest ask in the film is 1:50, where she
has to be genuinely excited about arguing over a text. Cast someone funny.

**Circle members get 3–5 seconds each.** Marcus, Priya and João are faces and
one gesture. João writes Portuguese so the translation layer appears naturally.

**What NOT to show.** No church interior, no crucifix, no candles, no hands
folded in prayer, no golden-hour lens flare over an open Bible. Every visual
cliché of the category is available and every one of them makes this film
generic. The room is a normal bedroom. The light is just light.

**What NOT to imply.** She was never failing. There is no shot of her looking
guilty at the closed book, and no beat where the app corrects her behaviour.

---

## 60-second cutdown

Keep: 0:00–0:26 (dark room, closes the book), 1:04–1:14 (the circle already
knows her name), 1:24–1:50 (prompts, passage, summary, the paper Bible
reopening), the wide daylight shot, end card.

Drop: the friend's link, onboarding, the digest, the final send.

The cutdown survives because the light arc survives. That is the test for any
edit of this film — if the brightness still climbs monotonically, the cut works.

## If this is the Kaggle submission video

**This is the one to shoot.** It needs no premise explained, it shows the most
product of any of the four scripts (icebreaker, pre-reading prompts, YouVersion
passage with version chip, lesson summary, thread, translation, digest), and its
argument — *people don't quit because they stop caring, they quit because
they're alone with a hard text* — is one a judge from either YouVersion or Gloo
will recognise immediately.

**Recommendation:** run V4 at **2:15** by trimming the bus beat at 0:26 to eight
seconds and tightening 1:50, then append **40 seconds** of silent screen capture
— Instant Access → passage → reflection → digest → Agent Console. Total ~2:55,
inside the three-minute limit.

**No adjacency risk.** V4 takes no position on streaks, habit tracking, or
anything the YouVersion Bible App does. The paper Bible reopening at 1:24 makes
the relationship explicit and generous: Round is not a replacement for reading
Scripture. It is the room you read it in.
