# Round — video script, version 5

**"Every Time I Looked Up"**
Runtime 2:20 · One continuous orbiting take · Near-wordless · Eight languages

---

## The swing

V1–V4 are naturalistic character dramas. This one is not. It is a single
unbroken camera move — the lens orbits a kitchen table for two minutes and
twenty seconds and never cuts. **Every time the camera passes behind the
protagonist's back and comes round again, someone new is sitting at the table.**

She starts alone in a pool of lamplight. She ends at a long table in open dawn
air with eleven people on it, all reading the same line in their own languages,
and the camera completes its last orbit to find her mid-sentence — talking, not
reading.

A circling camera, for a product called Round. The form is the argument.

**Be honest about the risk.** V5 is the only script here that can fail badly.
V4 executed at 70% is still a good ad. V5 executed at 70% is a student film. It
needs a camera operator who can carry a Steadicam orbit for two minutes, a
crew who can dress eleven people into a set silently between passes, and one
long shoot day. If you have those, this is the one people remember.

## Before you shoot

**Everyone on screen is a dramatization** performed by actors. End card as in
the other versions, uncut.

**This film shows the least product** of the five. That is a deliberate trade
and it must be paid for: append the 45-second silent screen capture after the
end card, non-negotiable, or a judge sees a mood piece and no working software.

**The words people speak are real product output.** The reflections they read
aloud should be written the way circle members actually write — specific, plain,
unpolished. The line spoken in unison at the peak is a digest line. Do not write
poetry for them; write what a nurse in Leeds would type at 11pm.

---

## The story in one line

She sat down to read alone, and every time she looked up there was one more
person at the table.

---

## The mechanic

One camera, one continuous orbit, clockwise, at seated eye height. It circles
the table at a steady rate — roughly **17 seconds per full revolution**, eight
revolutions across the film, accelerating slightly as it goes.

**The trick:** each time the lens passes behind **ANNA** — she is between camera
and table for about two seconds, her back filling frame — the crew places one
more person, one more chair, one more lamp. When the camera comes round, they
are simply there. No cut, no dissolve, no VFX.

Shoot it for real. It is an old theatre trick and it is far more affecting than
a visual effect because the audience cannot find the seam.

## Script — by revolution

### Rev 1 (0:00 – 0:17) — Alone

| | |
|---|---|
| **CAMERA** | Starts tight on a closed Bible on a kitchen table. Pulls back and begins to orbit. |
| **SET** | Near-black. One lamp. One chair. **ANNA** (33), coat on, reading. Her phone face-down beside the book. |
| **SOUND** | Room tone. A fridge hum. Her breathing. Nothing else. |
| **VO (Anna, barely above a whisper — the first of only four lines)** | I read the same verse four times. |

---

### Rev 2 (0:17 – 0:34) — One more chair

| | |
|---|---|
| **BEHIND HER BACK** | Add: one chair, occupied. **MARCUS** (60s), reading his own copy, not looking at her. |
| **CAMERA** | Comes round. Marcus is there. Anna does not react — she is reading. *We* react. |
| **SOUND** | A second breathing pattern enters the mix. Very low. |
| **DETAIL** | Anna's phone, face-down at Rev 1, is now face-up. On it, briefly legible as the camera passes: the icebreaker message from **Round**, naming her and naming Marcus. |

---

### Rev 3 (0:34 – 0:49) — A voice

| | |
|---|---|
| **BEHIND HER BACK** | Add: **JOÃO** (44) and a lamp. |
| **CAMERA** | Comes round. Three people. The lamp has raised the light one notch. |
| **SOUND** | João reads a line aloud, quietly, **in Portuguese.** He is not performing; he is reading to himself the way people do. |
| **ACTION** | Anna looks up for the first time. Not startled. Curious. She looks back down. |

> **Direction:** This is the film's first beat of contact and it must be
> underplayed to nothing. She glances up the way you do in a library.

### Rev 4 (0:49 – 1:03) — Overlap

| | |
|---|---|
| **BEHIND HER BACK** | Add: **PRIYA** (29), **SOFIA** (29), two more chairs. The table is visibly longer than it was. |
| **CAMERA** | Slightly faster now. |
| **SOUND** | Sofia reads in **Spanish**, overlapping João's Portuguese. Two languages, same rhythm, obviously the same words. |
| **ACTION** | Anna's finger stops on the page. She has noticed they are all on the same line. |
| **VO (Anna — line 2 of 4)** | They were on my verse. |

---

### Rev 5 (1:03 – 1:17) — The room gives way

| | |
|---|---|
| **BEHIND HER BACK** | Add: two more people. And — the back wall of the kitchen is gone. Beyond it, open air, pre-dawn blue. |
| **CAMERA** | The orbit now carries partly outside the room. |
| **SOUND** | The fridge hum is replaced by outdoor air. A bird. Wind. Four voices reading now, four languages, layered but never chaotic. |
| **ACTION** | Anna takes her coat off. Hangs it on the back of her chair. |

> **Direction:** The coat is the only thing she does in the entire film that
> signals a decision. Make it small and make sure the camera catches it.

---

### Rev 6 (1:17 – 1:33) — She speaks

| | |
|---|---|
| **BEHIND HER BACK** | Add: two more. Nine at the table. More lamps — the light is now clearly dawn, not lamplight. |
| **ACTION** | Anna says something. We do not hear the whole sentence, just the end of it, and it is a question — she is asking the table something about the text. |
| **SOUND** | Voices answer. Overlapping, warm, unresolved — an actual conversation, not a chorus. The first laugh in the film, from somebody off-camera. |

---

### Rev 7 (1:33 – 1:52) — In unison

| | |
|---|---|
| **BEHIND HER BACK** | Add: the last two. Eleven people. The table now runs off into the open air with no visible end. |
| **CAMERA** | Slowest, widest pass of the film. |
| **SOUND** | All eleven read **one line together**, each in their own language, not synchronised into a chant but arriving within a second of each other — the way a room reads aloud. |
| **ON SCREEN (only text card in the film, small, lower third)** | *Round's daily digest: what your circle noticed together.* |
| **LIGHT** | Full dawn. The brightest moment. |

> **Product truth:** the digest is a real thing the Facilitator Agent writes
> every day per circle — a synthesis of the members' own reflections, with an
> overlap callout naming who landed on the same theme. Eleven people saying one
> sentence in eight languages is a literal picture of what it does.

---

### Rev 8 (1:52 – 2:12) — Back to her

| | |
|---|---|
| **CAMERA** | Final orbit, returning to the exact opening framing — tight on the table in front of Anna. |
| **SET** | The Bible is open now. Her phone sits beside it, screen alive with the thread. |
| **ACTION** | Anna is mid-sentence, talking, gesturing with one hand, not reading at all. Off-camera, several people answer at once and someone interrupts her. She laughs and keeps going. |
| **VO (Anna — line 3 of 4)** | I sat down on my own. |
| **VO (line 4 of 4)** | I don't remember when that stopped being true. |
| **SOUND** | The full table, mid-argument, warm. Then — hard cut to silence. |

## Production notes

**The orbit.** Steadicam or a gimbal on a circular dolly track. Rehearse the
revolution rate to a metronome — 17 seconds per pass, tightening to about 14 by
Rev 7. The crew works to the same count. Budget a full day and expect twenty
takes; you are looking for one where nobody clips a chair leg.

**Placing people.** Two seconds of cover per pass is enough for one seated
person if they are pre-positioned crouching just out of frame. Socked feet, no
jewellery, no rustling fabric. Rehearse the placements silently before the
camera ever rolls.

**Cheating the wall.** Rev 5 removes the back wall. Cheapest honest method:
build the kitchen as a three-wall set on a location with a real view behind, and
fly the fourth wall out during the two-second cover. If you cannot fly it, this
is the one place to allow a VFX seam — hide it in the darkest part of the pass.

**The table growing.** Do not try to extend one table. Use nested tables of
increasing length, swapped and dressed identically. The audience tracks the
tablecloth and the lamps, not the joinery.

**Languages.** Eight, minimum: English, Portuguese, Spanish, Korean, Swahili,
Polish, Tagalog, Arabic. Cast native speakers and let them read naturally.
**Never subtitle them.** The audience does not need to know what is said — they
need to hear that it is the same thing.

**Light.** Practical only. One lamp at Rev 1, one added per revolution, and real
dawn from Rev 5 onward. Shoot the second half in the actual blue-to-gold window;
that is why this is a one-day, one-location, get-it-right shoot.

**Sound is half the film.** Build it as a score: one breath, two breaths, one
voice, two overlapping, four, a conversation, eleven in unison, then argument,
then silence. Composer brief: no music at all until Rev 5, and even then only
underneath the voices. The voices *are* the score.

**What NOT to do.** No slow-motion. No dissolve. No visible cut — if you have to
stitch, hide the stitch behind her back and tell no one. No candles, no church,
no golden-hair-lit open Bible. And do not have the group turn and smile at her;
they are reading, not welcoming a guest.

## 60-second cutdown

There isn't one. This film is a single move and cannot be shortened without
destroying the mechanic. If you need 60 seconds, shoot V4 instead.

That is a genuine limitation, not a purist objection — worth weighing if the
submission video also needs to work as social media cutdowns.

---

## If this is the Kaggle submission video

**Format:** V5 at 2:20 + hard cut + **35 seconds of silent screen capture**
(Instant Access → passage → reflection → digest → Agent Console) = **2:55**.
The screen capture is mandatory here in a way it is optional elsewhere, because
V5 deliberately shows almost no interface.

**Honest recommendation:** shoot **V4**. It is clearer, it shows the whole
product, it survives an imperfect execution, and a judge watching thirty
submissions will understand it in three seconds.

Shoot **V5** only if you have a real camera operator, a real location, and one
full day. If you do, it is the only one of the five that a judge will still be
describing to somebody a week later — and "the table that fills up behind her"
is a much easier thing to remember than any feature list.
