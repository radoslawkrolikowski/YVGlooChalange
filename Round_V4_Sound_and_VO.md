# Round – V4 Sound and Voiceover Build List

**The generated clips carry no usable audio.** Everything below is built in post:
ambience beds, foley, score, and every word spoken.

All timestamps are **relative to the start of that clip**, not the finished film.

---

## Summary of what to record or source

| Type | Count |
|---|---|
| Voiceover lines (over picture) | 6 |
| Diegetic spoken lines (on camera) | 2 |
| Ambience beds | 6 distinct |
| Foley items | 11 |
| Score cues | 6 stages |

**Record all voice in one session** — same mic, same distance, same room. Clip 2
is the first time the audience hears her, so the voiceover lines must match it or
the film gains a second actor by accident.

# 1. Voice

## Diegetic — spoken on camera, clip 2

Psalm 1:1, BSB. Public domain. She reads aloud, then repeats the opening of the
same clause.

| Clip | At | Text | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1.0 | *"…or sit in the seat of mockers."* | Low, slow, tired. Reading to herself, not performing. Trails off on the last word |
| 2 | 6.2 | *"or sit in the seat of…"* | Slower and flatter than the first pass. Cut off partway — she gives up mid-phrase |

**Perform quiet, mix loud.** Close mic at 10–15cm, murmured delivery, then bring
it to −12 to −18 dBFS with room tone 30–40 dB below. A loud performance mixed low
sounds like the next room; a quiet one mixed high sounds like you are beside her.
Add a little reverb matched to the bedroom.

**Test on a phone speaker.** If you cannot hear that the second reading repeats
the first, it is too quiet — and the repetition is the entire beat.

| Clip | At | Text | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 3.5 | *"I didn't understand it."* | Flat. A verdict, not a complaint. No self-pity |
| 3 | 5.2 | *"And there was nobody to ask."* | Same, quieter. Let it land and stop |
| 5 | 1.5 | *"It didn't tell me anything. It asked me something."* | Quiet surprise. The first lift in the film — small, not bright |
| 6 | 2.0 | *"Four people I'd never met. And the first thing that happened was somebody said my name."* | Warm, faintly disbelieving. Still guarded |
| 9 | 2.5 | *"Same book. I just wasn't on my own with it any more."* | Settled. Quiet relief, no emphasis on any word |
| 11–13 | 11 @ 1.5 | *"I started looking forward to it. Not the reading. The arguing about it."* | Light, wry, almost amused. The only near-smile in the voice |
| 15 | 2.5 | *"Ada and Marcus had stopped in the same place."* | Plain observation. Reporting something she watched happen |
| 15 | 4.2 | *"Neither of them knew until Round told them."* | Slightly awed, still plain. Do not sell it |

**Record three passes of everything** — one flat, one warmer, one almost
whispered. You will use different passes early and late, because the point of the
film is that she changes. Clips 3 and 5 come from the flat pass; 11–13 and 15
from the warmer one.

**Clip 15 is the only line that explains a mechanism** rather than a feeling.
Read it plainer than the rest so it sits as observation, not sales copy.

**No voiceover anywhere in the demo tail.** The silence there is the gear change
from film to evidence.

# 2. Ambience beds

Six distinct environments. Lay each as a continuous bed under its clips, then
duck for voice.

| Bed | Clips | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Bedroom night** | 1, 2, 3 | Low hum from elsewhere in the flat (fridge-ish). Muffled distant traffic, indistinct. No rhythm — a flat hum, never a ticking clock or radiator |
| **Bus interior** | 4, 5, 6, 7 | Engine hum, road noise, faint window rattle. Continuous, unremarkable |
| **Bedroom morning** | 8a, 8b, 9, 10a, 10b | Quieter than the bus. Faint birdsong outside the window. No traffic |
| **Street exterior** | 11 | Open-street air, distant traffic, light wind. Bright and unenclosed |
| **Kitchen interior** | 12 | Quiet interior room tone, close and domestic |
| **Balcony** | 13 | Distant city hum from below, light wind, one or two birds. Airier than the kitchen |
| **Bedroom day, window open** | 16, 17, 18 | Birdsong and real street noise coming *in* through the open window — the world arriving in the room |

**No ticking anywhere.** A ticking clock or radiator implies waiting and pulls the
film toward thriller. She is not waiting for anything — that is the problem.

# 4. Score

**No music at all until clip 4.** Clips 1–3 are room tone and voice only. That
silence is what gives the first note somewhere to arrive.

| Stage | Clip | At | What |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | 6.0 | **One low sustained note**, entering as light crosses her face on the bus. The first music in the film |
| 2 | 5 | 3.0 | A second, softer note joins as she starts typing. Resolving warmer by 6.0 |
| 3 | 6 | 0.0 | Rhythm enters. The film starts moving |
| 4 | 7–10a | — | Gentle score continuing, warming through the bedroom morning |
| 5 | 11–13 | — | Building to the **peak at clip 12** — full, bright, unembarrassed |
| 6 | 15–18 | — | Sustains through the digest, then resolves across 17 and **fades to silence** at the end of 18 |

**Cut hard to silence on the last frame of 18**, before the demo tail.

# 5. Mix notes

**Duck the ambience under every voice line**, but keep it audible — she is always
somewhere, never in a vacuum.

**Clip 2 has no music at all**, which is what makes the whispered repetition
readable. Do not let the score creep in early to fill the space.

**Clip 15's two-second hold (3.0–5.0)** is the most important pause in the film.
Score sustains, street noise underneath, nothing else. Resist adding a swell.

**The demo tail is silent.** No music, no voiceover, captions only.

---

# 6. What to record yourself vs. source

**Record:**
- All 8 voice lines, three passes each
- The thumb-on-dust brush (clip 1)
- The door click (clip 8a)
- Her laugh (clip 12) — record it with the voice session, same mic

**Source or library:**
- All six ambience beds
- Traffic, birdsong, kettle, footsteps, page turns, keyboard taps
- Score

Page turns and keyboard taps are worth recording if you have the real props to
hand — the paper Bible and the phone are already on set for the practical clips,
and matched foley beats a library take every time.
