# Round – V4 Production Guide

**"Open the Curtains"** · 20 clips · ~1:53 narrative + ~40s demo tail

Each clip below is a **self-contained card**: who shoots it, framing, action,
light, what's in frame, sound, voiceover, and the one note that matters. Nothing
about a clip lives anywhere else.

Read the four **Before you start** blocks once. Then work clip by clip.

---

## The rule

> **Anything showing a screen without a face is filmed practically.
> Everything else is AI-generated.**

Real hands beat AI hands, real screens beat comped screens. AI keeps everything
carrying her face, which is what it is actually good at.

**12 AI · 8 practical.**

## Before you start 1 — the light ramp

**The spine of the film: every clip is brighter than the one before it. No
resets, no dips for drama.** The audience will not consciously notice and will
feel it completely.

| Clips | Where | Level |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Bedroom, night | Near-black. One warm practical lamp |
| 4 | Bus, morning | Dim, cold, overcast |
| 5 | Bus | Phone glow — first warm source in the film |
| 6–7 | Bus | Cool, rising |
| 8a–10b | Bedroom, next morning | Warm window light, curtains half open, climbing across the five clips |
| 11–13 | Three locations, day | Bright, varied. Clip 13 carries the first direct sun |
| 14 | Desk or similar, day | Bright — same family as 11–13 |
| 15 | Day, standing | Bright, cool daylight |
| 16 | Bedroom | Brighter still, curtains full |
| 17 | Bedroom | **Brightest frame in the film.** Direct sun |
| 18 | Bedroom | Bright |

**One source, always.** No overheads, no mixed colour temperature — a window
with a net curtain, or one bounced lamp. Two sources create a shadow signature
your AI clips will not have.

## Before you start 2 — continuity lock

The hybrid fails at the joins. Lock these before writing a single prompt.

**Reference sheet** — generate this once, before anything else, and use it as the
image reference on all 12 AI clips:

> Portrait of a 30-year-old white woman, neutral expression, looking directly at
> camera. Oval face, soft jawline, light freckles scattered across the bridge of
> her nose and upper cheeks only — light density, not heavy. Grey-green eyes,
> pale eyelashes. Fair skin with visible natural texture and pores, faint shadows
> under the eyes, no makeup. Dark blonde hair, shoulder length, tied back loosely
> at the nape with a few loose strands at the temples. Slim build, average
> height. No jewellery, no watch, no nail polish. Plain mid-grey backdrop, soft
> even frontal light, no dramatic shadows. Photographic, natural, unretouched.
> Shot on 50mm, shallow depth of field.

Three views if your tool supports it — front, three-quarter, profile, same
lighting across all three.

**Negative prompt:** `makeup, glamour, beauty retouching, airbrushed skin,
smiling, model pose, jewellery, earrings, heavy freckles, perfect symmetry,
fashion photography, studio glamour lighting`

**Character block** — paste verbatim into all 12 AI prompts, no variation:

> 30-year-old white woman, oval face, light freckles across the nose and upper
> cheeks, grey-green eyes, dark blonde hair shoulder length tied back loosely, no
> makeup, no jewellery, no nail polish, slim build, tired eyes.

**Wardrobe block** — kept separate from the character block, because it changes
and the change is a story beat:

| Clips | Wardrobe |
|---|---|
| 1–8a | Grey oversized wool coat, worn open over a plain dark long-sleeve top |
| 8a | Coat over the back of the chair; she is in the dark long-sleeve top |
| 8b–16 | Dark long-sleeve top, **sleeves pushed to mid-forearm** |
| 17 | Coat on a hook by the door, no one in frame |

The pushed-back sleeves matter: clips 7, 8b, 9, 10b, 14 and 15 are practical
(clip 7 keeps the coat sleeve, not pushed up — see its wardrobe stage above),
so your own forearms have to match what the model generates.

**Four things that will save regenerations:**

- **Keep the hair tied back in every shot.** Loose hair is the most variable
  feature across generations — length, parting and volume drift constantly.
  Tied back is far more stable, and it reads as "came home from work"
- **Check the freckles on every generation.** Models default to either none or a
  heavy dusting. *Bridge of the nose and upper cheeks only, light density* holds
  much better than "freckles," and this is the feature most likely to break the
  illusion between clips
- **Always say "visible natural texture and pores."** Without it you get plastic
  skin, and plastic skin at 30 reads as 22
- **Never say "beautiful" or "attractive."** It pushes hard toward a generic
  model face. You want someone who looks like she has a job and a commute

**Location block** — paste into every bedroom prompt:

> Small bedroom, white walls, unmade grey duvet, dark wood nightstand, brass
> bedside lamp with a cream shade, window camera-left with grey linen curtains.

**Hands** — your practical hands must match the AI hands. Do not try to match a
manicure across a diffusion model. Lock **no rings, no watch, no bracelet, no
nail polish, plain sleeve pushed to mid-forearm**, and put that phrasing in
every AI prompt containing hands.

**Props** — buy or dress these first, then describe *your actual props* in the
prompts rather than the reverse: the Bible (cover, size, page-edge colour), the
phone (model, case, colour), the coat, the duvet, the nightstand, the lamp.

**Order of work.** Approve the AI clips **first**, then shoot the practical
inserts to match what exists. Matching to a guess means reshooting. **Grade
practical to AI, never the reverse** — the AI look is harder to move.

**Cut on motion at every AI→practical seam.** Land the cut mid-gesture, hand
already moving into frame. A cut on a static frame invites comparison.

## Before you start 3 — screen treatment

Decide before generating; it changes the prompts.

| Treatment | Clips | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Practical** | 6, 7, 8b, 9, 10b, 14, 15 | Real phone, real screen, filmed. No compositing |
| **Pane** | 4, 11–13, 18 | Screen faces away in generation; UI added in post as a floating card |
| **Glow only** | 5 | No screen, no pane — just its light on her face |
| **No screen** | 1, 2, 3, 8a, 10a, 16, 17 | Phone absent, face-down, or blank |

**Prompt phrasing for screen-away clips.** The model will swing the screen into
frame and render nonsense on it unless forbidden: *"phone held with the back of
the device toward camera, screen not visible, hand gripping the lower edge."*

**Pane specification — static image, made in Canva, no tracking.** Each pane is
one flat PNG designed in Canva and placed once as a static overlay for the
duration it's visible. No motion tracking, no parallax — the phone barely moves
in these shots beyond a gentle handheld sway, so a fixed position holds up. This
replaces any earlier plan to track the pane to the device in 3D.

**Composite steps, per clip:**
1. Design the pane in Canva (see below for exact content per clip).
2. Export as **PNG with a transparent background**, shadow baked in at export.
3. Drop it into the timeline at a fixed position over the AI clip — roughly where
   the phone sits in frame, offset slightly above or beside it, never on top of
   the device itself.
4. Opacity **90–95%** — "a bit transparent" fights the legibility you're buying.
5. **Fade 6 frames in, 6 frames out**, timed to when her eyes go to the phone and
   away from it (see each clip's timing beats).
6. If the phone moves noticeably during the shot, reposition the pane with two or
   three manual keyframes rather than building a tracked animation — this is a
   static-asset workflow, not a match-move.

**Same corner, same scale, same fade, every time.** First appearance is clip 4 —
it sets the grammar. If the first pane a viewer sees arrives at clip 15, it reads
as a patch rather than a language.

### Building the panes in Canva

**Canvas.** Custom size, phone-screen ratio — **1080 × 2340px** (standard modern
phone). Design the full screen, not just a cropped card; crop tighter at export
if a clip needs a smaller card instead of a full screen.

**Two visual styles, not one** — do not mix them:

- **Native Messages app** (clips 4, 18) — plain iOS/Android-style message
  bubbles. Grey/white incoming bubble, blue or green outgoing bubble, sender name
  small at the top, system font, white or dark background. Nothing app-branded.
- **Round app UI** (clips 11–13) — take real screenshots from
  https://yv-gloo-chalange.vercel.app as your base layer, then edit only the text
  needed in Canva. This keeps the typography, spacing and colour exactly as the
  real product renders it, which matters more here than in the Messages clips —
  a judge who opens the live app will compare them.

**Export.** PNG, transparent background outside the phone-screen shape, soft
drop shadow added in Canva before export (Canva's built-in shadow effect on the
image group is enough — no need to fake it in the compositor).

**No real text anywhere gets typed from scratch that could look AI-garbled.**
Canva renders real fonts correctly, so this problem only existed for the AI
video generations — the panes are exempt. Still keep every string short and
proofread; a legible typo is worse than an illegible AI one.

### Pane content, clip by clip

| Clip | Style | What to design |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | Messages app | Incoming bubble from a contact named **Priya**, no photo. Message text: *"This one's different."* Below it, a link preview card with the Round app's title and URL. Timestamp small, above the bubble |
| **11** | Round app UI | Thread, **Ada's** message. Cropped to the single bubble |
| **12** | Round app UI | Thread, **Marcus's** reflection. Cropped to its first line |
| **13** | Round app UI | Thread, **João's** message in the Portuguese original. Cropped to the single bubble |
| **18** | Messages app | Outgoing bubble (right-aligned, blue/green), same text and link preview as clip 4's incoming one — she is sending on what she received. *"Delivered"* small beneath it |

Clips 11–13 are three **different** screenshots, not one scrolled — each shows a
different point in the thread so the three locations don't appear to be looking
at an identical screen. **Clips 7 and 14 have no pane row** — both are filmed
practically, see their own cards.

### Crop the montage panes tight

**Clips 4 and 18 use the full phone screen** — the link preview is part of what
those beats are showing.

**Clips 11–13 do not.** Crop each to the single message bubble. At pane scale in
a 3–4 second clip, a full screen renders as a grey block of text-shaped noise —
decoration rather than information. One message, large, legible is the difference
between a viewer reading *"Ada: I keep coming back to the image of the tree…"*
and seeing a smudge.

Marcus's reflection in clip 12 is the longest of the three; crop to its opening
line, which is where the hook is anyway.

### Pane visibility timings

The pane fades in when her eyes go to the phone and out before the clip ends. Six
frames each way. **Full visibility is what matters** — below about two seconds a
short message cannot be read.

| Clip | Length | Fade in | Fully visible | Fade out | Visible for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **4** | 8s | 2.0 | 2.25 → 6.0 | 6.0 | **3.75s** |
| **11** | 4s | 0.8 | 1.05 → 3.4 | 3.4 | **2.35s** |
| **12** | 4s | 0.8 | 1.05 → 3.4 | 3.4 | **2.35s** |
| **13** | 4s | 0.8 | 1.05 → 3.4 | 3.4 | **2.35s** |
| **18** | 6s | 1.5 | 1.75 → 3.5 | 3.5 | **1.75s** |

**Clips 11–13 were 3s and grew to 4s for this reason.** At 3s the pane is fully
visible for only ~1.85s, which registers *that* a message arrived but not what it
says. The extra second per clip costs 3s across the montage and is the difference
between the panes carrying information and being wallpaper.

**Clip 18 is deliberately short at 1.75s** — the audience already read that exact
message in clip 4. It only needs to be recognised, not read.

**If you move clips 11–13 to practical too** (see the shot list note), this
table empties out entirely — you'd film the real screen instead of designing it.

### What never goes in a generation prompt

**Quoted text of any kind.** A quoted passage in the prompt gets the generation
**rejected on copyright grounds** — the filter flags quoted material without
checking provenance. This happened on clip 2 with a public-domain Bible verse.
Describe the *action*: "she reads under her breath, mouth moving, fewer words the
second time." The words arrive in post, from your own recording.

**The word "Bible."** It summons crosses, candles, church interiors and
gold-embossed covers — every cliché the film avoids — plus lettering that renders
as garbage. Say **"a closed book with a plain dark cover."** Nobody needs the
title legible; context does the work.

**Any body text you want readable.** Models render text as garbled
pseudo-lettering. Where a page is in frame, prompt it *out* of legibility:
*"text soft and out of focus, readable as texture only, never legible."* Real
readable text only ever appears in the practical clips, on real screens and real
paper.

**Brand names**, including film stocks if a generation gets refused. `35mm film,
natural grain` is safer than naming a stock.

**AI-look accelerants** — `cinematic`, `hyperdetailed`, `ultra realistic`, `8k`,
`4k`, `masterpiece`, `sharp focus`, `HDR`, `beautiful lighting`. Use capture
language instead: `35mm film`, `available light only`, `shallow depth of field`,
`natural grain`, `slightly underexposed`, `flat contrast`, `handheld`, `candid`.

### Lean prompt template

The character and location are supplied as **reference images**, so prompts carry
only what those cannot:

```
1. Lighting  — always, in several phrasings. Models drift bright regardless of
               a dark reference image
2. Wardrobe stage — one clause: "she still has her coat on" / "coat over the
               chair" / "sleeves pushed to mid-forearm"
3. Props the reference does not hold — the book, the dust, the phone's position
4. Camera    — framing and whether it moves. State "one continuous move, no cuts"
5. Timings   — beat by beat, with seconds
6. Sound     — layers and their timings
```

No face description, no room inventory. Both are already in the references and
repeating them competes with what the images are telling the model.

## Before you start 4 — filming screens

For the eight practical clips and the demo tail.

**Frame tight.** If the frame holds **hands, phone, one surface**, that is the
entire list of things that has to match. No room, no wall, no furniture, no face.
Clip 9 is the only exception and even there, crop to the surface.

**One phone, one orientation, one colour scheme** for everything. Do Not Disturb
on. Neutral clock, kept consistent. Native resolution, 60fps. Wired capture via
QuickTime beats on-device recording.

**Lock screen brightness manually** — auto-brightness drifts mid-take and there
is no fixing it in post.

**Watch for banding.** Camera shutter beating against display refresh gives
rolling dark bars. Start at 1/60 or 1/50, check playback at 100% before
committing.

**Kill reflections.** Angle the phone 10–15° off perpendicular, wear dark
clothing, keep the source off-axis. A polariser helps.

**Hold one second of stillness before and after every tap.** Those frames are
your cut points. **Scroll at half the speed that feels natural.** Record
everything three times.

## Clip 2 — The wall

**AI · 12s · no screen** — room reference: **night** · **Status: ✅ generated and approved**

- **Frame** — Over the shoulder, wide enough to hold both the open page and the phone on the nightstand. Camera almost static, barely perceptible drift in
- **Action** — Finger tracks under a line as she reads aloud. Voice trails off. Finger returns to the start of the *same* line, she reads it again — slower, flatter, cut off partway. Small frown. **Then: reaches toward the phone to look something up, stops with her hand halfway, withdraws it**
- **Light** — Near-black. The lamp beam now falls on **the open page**, which is the brightest thing in frame; her face is lit only by bounce off the page
- **In frame** — The dark-covered book open in her lap, phone **face-down** on the nightstand. **Page text soft and out of focus, never legible** — focus on her face and hand
- **Sound** — See the spoken line below. Room tone, low hum, muffled traffic. No music
- **VO** — none *(but she speaks — see below)*

**SPOKEN LINE — recorded, not generated**

She reads Psalm 1:1 (BSB) aloud, then repeats the opening of the same clause:

> **1.0s** — *"…or sit in the seat of mockers."* — low, slow, tired, trailing off on the last word
>
> **6.2s** — *"or sit in the seat of…"* — slower, flatter, cut off partway

Direct the actor with the verse. **Do not put the words in the generation prompt** —
see the copyright-filter note in *Before you start 3*. Generate her with mouth
moving, replace the audio in post.

Why this line: the triple structure (walk / stand / sit) reads as repetitive
rather than deliberate until someone explains it — which is exactly what the
commentary corpus and the lesson summary do. **The line she cannot get through
here is the line she understands in clip 10a.**

**Perform quiet, mix loud.** Close mic at 10–15cm, murmured delivery, then bring
it up to −12 to −18 dBFS with room tone 30–40 dB below. A loud performance mixed
low sounds like the next room; a quiet one mixed high sounds like you are sitting
beside her. Add a little reverb matched to the bedroom so it sits in the space.
**Test on a phone speaker** — if you cannot hear that the second reading repeats
the first, it is too quiet, and the repetition is the entire beat.

- **Note** — **The phone gesture is the most important beat in the film and must not be cut.** Without it this is a tired woman who gave up; with it, the scene says *there was nobody to ask*, which is the premise Round answers. Two prompt-craft points: "reads the same line twice" is not renderable — the **finger** carries the repetition, not her eyes, because models render fingers reliably and micro eye movement badly. And the page must never be legible; AI renders body text as garbled pseudo-lettering, which is why the **audio** carries the words instead

## Clip 8b — Prompts, passage, highlight

**PRACTICAL · 8s · real screen**

- **Frame** — Hands and phone, slight top-down
- **Action** — Real app, real taps:
  1. (0.0–2.0) Page has just loaded. A brief **"Preparing your prompts…"**
     shimmer plays under the **"Just for you"** header, then 2–3 short prompts
     populate. **The card is already open — it does not need expanding**
  2. (2.0–3.5) Thumb scrolls down past the prompts card to the passage.
     Version chip visible beside the reference
  3. (3.5–5.5) **Long-press** a word partway through the passage, then drag the
     selection handles to extend across one full line
  4. (5.5–6.5) A small **"Highlight"** pill button (highlighter icon) appears
     floating just above the selected text
  5. (6.5–7.5) Thumb taps that button. The selection clears and the phrase
     washes into the highlight colour, staying marked
  6. (7.5–8.0) Thumb lifts. Hold on the highlighted line
- **Light** — **Warm-neutral, soft, single window direction. Screen now roughly matches ambient rather than beating it**
- **In frame** — Hands, phone, duvet. Version chip visible on screen
- **Sound** — Fabric, a breath, the soft tap of the Highlight button
- **VO** — none
- **Note** — Practical rather than pane because this is a real multi-step
  interaction — select, a button appears in response, confirm — not a single
  gesture. A static image can't show a live text selection or a button

# Demo tail — ~40 seconds

Runs after the end card. **Hard cut, no music, no voiceover.** Its only job is to
prove the product is real and works — judges have seen a lot of beautiful videos
attached to nothing. The silence is deliberate: it signals a gear change from
film to evidence.

**Clean screen capture, not a filmed phone.** The digest's "Shared ground" block and the version
chip are small type; filming a screen costs the resolution that makes them
readable, and a hard cut from warm bedroom to pixel-perfect capture *is* the gear
change. Optional 2-second bridge: her hand setting the phone down, then cut.

| Time | Show | Why it earns the seconds |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5s | Landing → **Instant Access** → into the app | No sign-up wall. The barrier is genuinely zero |
| 5–11s | Language + version picker. **Spanish → RVES** | The version-ID mechanism |
| 11–17s | **Pre-reading prompts**, then the passage in Spanish, version chip visible, **Open in Bible App** in frame | Live YouVersion fetch in the reader's language, deep-linking back to the Bible App |
| 17–23s | Drag-highlight a line, finish reading, **conversation starters** appear | Highlights feeding the next AI call |
| 23–29s | Share a reflection (button reads **"Share"**, not "Post") → thread → a **translated message** with the **Show original** toggle | The multilingual layer, hardest thing to convey in prose |
| 29–35s | The **digest**'s "Shared ground" block (avatar chips + theme phrase), and the **lesson summary** expanded | The Facilitator and Summary agents — the product thesis |
| 35–40s | **Prayer** tab: a shared request showing a **count** and no names | The one mechanism nothing else has. Let the number sit |

**Captions, not narration.** Small lower-third labels so it tracks on mute:
*Instant Access — no account* · *Passage live from YouVersion* · *Pre-reading
prompts — Gloo* · *Translated by Round* · *Daily digest — Facilitator Agent* ·
*Prayed for by a count, never a name*.

**Vertical capture in a 16:9 timeline** leaves empty columns. Put the captions
there — it uses the dead area and looks deliberate.

### Three things that will bite you

**Version licensing.** Only one version per language is licensed to this app key:
**BSB** (English), **RVES** (Spanish), **BLT** (Portuguese). The rest appear
disabled. Pick a licensed one on camera.

**The Agent Console is 404 in production.** `/dev/agents` is gated off the live
deployment by design. It is the strongest evidence shot available — every agent,
its prompt, the routed model, the log row — so record it from a **local dev
server** or a **preview deployment**. Budget 8 extra seconds at the end.

**Refresh the demo data first**, so the digest is same-day and the thread looks
current:

```
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $CRON_SECRET" \
  https://yv-gloo-chalange.vercel.app/api/cron/daily
```

That sweep also prunes anonymous session data — record *after* it, not before.
